Chapter one · The name

A name from the borderlands

Retzke is a rare surname. Only a few hundred people carry it worldwide, roughly split between Germany and the United States. It comes from the lands east of the Elbe River, where Slavic-speaking peoples lived before German settlers arrived in the 1100s. The two languages mixed there for centuries.

The ending -ke is a Low German diminutive, roughly "little." The stem Retz- is less certain. It may be a short form of a Germanic name like Konrad, a Germanized Slavic name built on rad ("glad"), or a village name such as Retzow or Retzin. Speculation

Little Retz, or "the one from Retzin": the name probably began as a nickname in a small village.
Chapter two · The homeland

Where the Retzkes probably came from

Paul George Retzke Sr. was born in Germany in 1881. No record found so far names his town. These are the strongest candidates, with the reasoning for each.

Farther Pomerania

Once Prussian Pomerania · now northwest Poland

Paul's sisters married men named Pahnke and Maass, both typical Pomeranian surnames. About 243,000 Pomeranians left for America between 1850 and 1900. Speculation

Retzin

Pomerania · now Rzecino, Poland

A village whose name matches the Retz- stem. If the surname means "the one from Retzin", this could be the family's root. No family link has been found. Speculation

Retztow

Pomerania · now Redostowo, Poland

Another look-alike place name in the same region. These names now appear in Polish archives under their Polish forms. Speculation

Retzow, Havelland

Brandenburg, Germany

A Brandenburg village whose name is said to come from a Slavic word for lynx. Modern Retzkes cluster in Brandenburg, around Potsdam. Speculation

Wolfen

Saxony-Anhalt, Germany

Birthplace of the Olympic heptathlete Astrid Retzke. It shows that the name survives in eastern Germany; it is not evidence of a link to this family. Record

Chapter three · Why they left

The great German exodus

The Retzkes probably left during the biggest wave of German emigration in history. These are the forces that drove most families like theirs.

3M
Germans left, 1862–1890Almost three million emigrated while Bismarck was in power. Most went to the United States. German History in Documents
1880s
The largest waveAfter 1880, economic swings set off the biggest surge. It did not slow until the mid-1890s. Paul was born in 1881, right at its start.
20%
Pomerania emptiedAbout 243,000 Pomeranians left for America, and the rural population fell by about a fifth, while big estates took over the land. Landesmuseum MV
✉
Letters homeLow wages and scarce land pushed people out. Letters and money sent home by relatives already in America pulled them over, along with steamship advertising.

For a farm laborer on a Prussian estate, owning land at home was nearly impossible. In America land was cheap and wages were high. A family with ten children, like Paul's parents, had strong reasons to go. Speculation

Chapter four · The crossing

Who came, and when

No passenger record has been found yet. This is the most likely sequence based on what is known.

  1. 1881

    Paul George Retzke is born in Germany

    He is one of about ten children. Brother Frank Albert Carl follows in 1882. Likely

  2. 1882–1890

    The family sails as a group

    Paul and Frank were both born in Germany, and all the siblings appear in Ohio records. So the parents most likely crossed with young children in the 1880s, sailing from Hamburg or Bremerhaven in steerage. The trip took about two weeks by steamship. Speculation

  3. Arrival

    New York or Baltimore

    Before Ellis Island opened in 1892, immigrants to New York were processed at Castle Garden. Many North German Lloyd ships went to Locust Point, Baltimore, where a railroad ran west to Ohio. Speculation

  4. by 1900

    Settled in northern Ohio

    The 1900 census shows Paul in Ohio with his siblings, including Henry and Reinhardt. His sister Bertha's son Alfred Maass was born in Cleveland in February 1900. Likely

  5. 1907

    Paul marries a Sandusky schoolteacher

    Paul married Lora Anna Bing, born 1882 in Ohio. The couple set up home in Toledo. Record

Chapter five · The Ohio generations

Making a life in the Glass City

In the early 1900s Toledo was booming on glass, auto parts and the Great Lakes trade, and German immigrants had shaped the city since the mid-1800s.

Paul George Retzke Sr.

1881, Germany – 1976, Toledo

The immigrant. In 1935 and 1940 he lived in Toledo with sons Carl and Roy. He lived to about 95. Likely

Lora Anna (Bing) Retzke

1882, Ohio – c. 1967

She lived in Sandusky and taught at the Perkins and Margaretta schools in 1901–02. After marrying she taught in Toledo Public Schools. One of her school notebooks is held at the Follett House Museum. Record

George J. Bing

A Sandusky carriage maker and early aviator, killed in a car accident on September 23, 1914. His daughter Myrtle became known as "the girl evangelist from Ohio." Record

The family claim to fame

Uncle Carl named Mountain Dew

"Mountain dew" was old Scots-Irish slang for moonshine. According to Wikipedia, using it as the name of a soda was first suggested by Carl E. Retzke at an Owens-Illinois meeting in Toledo. Its source is a Toledo Blade article titled "He named the Dew," from November 13, 1999. Record

Owens-Illinois was Toledo's giant glass-bottle maker, so a meeting about a new drink's bottles makes sense. Some retellings call Carl an executive, but that is unconfirmed. Speculation

Chapter six · Roy's family

Roy, and the road to Janice, Jean and Jim

Roy E. Retzke was born in Toledo on November 22, 1921, the youngest son of an immigrant father and a schoolteacher mother. At 18 he still lived on Western Avenue with his father and his brother Carl. Likely

Roy married, and the couple had three children: Janice, Jean and James (Jim). The marriage ended in divorce, and Roy later remarried. Family

Roy died in Toledo on January 22, 2007. The family remembers his death as around 2008. The only other Toledo Retzke who died in 2008 was his nephew Paul, which supports Roy E. as the right man. Likely

From a village east of the Elbe to a house on Western Avenue took one generation. Janice, Jean and Jim are the second generation born in America.
